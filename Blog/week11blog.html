An ethical Internet

An ethical internet would mean that privacy is included as a core component. Which means that there is limited, intentional and clear data collection made available. Surveillance capitalism profits would no longer be a thing due to users not being handled as products but being treated as customers instead. Shoshana Zuboff, in The Age of Surveillance Capitalism, exposes the exploitation of human experiences by corporations in predictive behaviour and the use of profit. In an ethical model, users have full control over their data and have an understanding of how their information is used, stored, and shared amongst other users and platforms. Furthermore, an ethical internet requires accountability and transparency of algorithms. Today, bias is deeply embedded in every established community including job applications and social media content. Safiya Umoja Noble’s work Algorithms of Oppression discusses how many search engines hide the reflection and development of racism and sexism. In order to achieve having systems that are more equitable and impartial in discriminatory systems, algorithms need to experience bias inspections that involve transparency in the decision making process. Platforms should take accountability and be responsible for reducing the risk of harm especially in the light of misinformation and harassment. An ethical internet will embed legal rules and implications for the harm they cause while also protecting free speech with moderation practices. Lastly, an ethical internet supports the reorganisation and transparency of standards and aids efforts such as open-source movement meaning a group of programers growing and distributing source code and making it available to the public while also focusing on net neutrality and the third generation of the World Wide Web that will empower and authorise its users.
