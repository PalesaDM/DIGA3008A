Readings

“The Library of Babel” by Jorge Luis Borges (1941)

In The Library of Babel, Borges explores the idea of a library that contains unlimited information and endless variations of books. This library symbolizes overwhelming complexity and boundless knowledge. It closely mirrors our experience with the internet today—vast amounts of information spread across countless platforms. However, not all of this information is reliable or useful. Critical thinking becomes essential to sift through the noise and find content that is meaningful and accurate. While it’s impossible to read or understand everything, the skill lies in navigating the web effectively to uncover the most relevant insights.

The History of the Web

What stood out to me while reading about the history of the web is that it’s an ongoing, ever-evolving process. Technologies can rise quickly and fall just as fast due to new innovations. The web is a constantly shifting environment, which means developers must be flexible, open to learning, and ready to adapt. It’s fascinating to see how rapidly technology has advanced—and it will only continue to do so. With this in mind, it's important to anticipate future trends, especially those that can enhance performance, security, and user experience.

My Goals for Interactive Media

My main goal for this course is to gain a deeper understanding of the internet and how technology works. I want to learn how to code with both comprehension and confidence, and to apply this knowledge meaningfully. I aim to stay engaged in discussions and learn from my peers. Finally, I want to manage academic pressure in a healthy way so I can consistently deliver strong, thoughtful work—and achieve good results.
