Week 10 - Blog Post

29 April 2025

Summary

Spyros Kasapis’s article “Internet Access is a Fundamental Right” argues that access to the internet should be considered a fundamental right—especially in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic. The pandemic exposed digital inequality and widened the gap between those with and without access.

Methodology

Kasapis highlights how the pandemic revealed a previously unnoticed digital divide. Internet access became essential for information, education, health, and freedom of expression—all recognized human rights. The internet also became a vital platform for updates during lockdowns. Referencing Salvador Allende’s notion that happiness is a human right, Kasapis extends this idea to argue the same for internet access.

Key Arguments and Analysis

The shift to online platforms during the pandemic for work, school, shopping, and social connection emphasizes how embedded the internet is in daily life. While those with access can adjust easily, those without are excluded from critical opportunities. Kasapis refers to the UN's 2016 stance that rights online should reflect those offline.

Economic inequality remains a barrier. Kasapis argues for government recognition of internet access as a right to promote equity and inclusion.

Critical Reflection

Kasapis presents a strong argument, but practical challenges must be considered—such as funding, infrastructure, and maintenance. Additionally, prioritizing internet access should not overshadow urgent global issues like food insecurity, healthcare, and shelter. These basic needs must also remain central.

Additional Research

The European Parliament and the Indian Supreme Court both support the view that internet access is a fundamental right. They argue it enables the exercise of other rights. Looking ahead, focus should be placed on affordability, universal infrastructure, and strong data privacy protections in the digital age.
