Justice and ethics in your artistic and programming practice

With the background that I currently have of digital media, interactive media and animation, I have learnt that every single aesthetic choice I make, and every line of code carries weight and matters. I used focus on the functionality of my code with relief that it at least works but we need to remind ourselves that even code tells a story, it outlines your thought process and has its own uniqueness and authenticity to it. It is important to remember that technologies do not just function but frame in an ethical manner. The most obvious ethical programming practice is to not copy and paste lines of code from artificial intelligence systems but to use these systems appropriately. How do you use these appropriately? By using AI as a tool to guide you in programming and not control or think for you and by referencing whatever external sources used to highlight that a piece of work is not originally yours. The same applies with visuals that you may utilize across your website pages that are from external sources. A reading that changed my perspective is "Decolonizing the Machine Race, Gender and Disability in Robots and Algorithmic Art"(Abramovic et al., 2021). Abramovic and others argue that AI systems and algorithmic creative pieces usually strengthen brutal norms especially centring around masculinity and this was a quick wakeup call that aesthetics are never neutral because there will always be a hidden negative agenda behind the beauty. As I commence to make more projects in Interactive Media and Animation in future, I will always make it a mission to question and verify the sources of data I use and the work I reproduce as well avoid using AI tools that give data without consent.

References

Abramovic, B. et al., 2021. Decolonizing the Machine: Race, Gender and Disability in Robots and Algorithmic Art. Benjamin, R., 2019. Race After Technology: Abolitionist Tools for the New Jim Code. Cambridge: Polity Press.
