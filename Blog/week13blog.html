Design Justice and the Internet

The two articles I chose for this blog are: “Design Justice” by Sasha Costanza-Chock (2020) and “Algorithms of Oppression” by Safiya Noble (2018)

Design Justice

This reading by Sasha Costanza gave me an understanding of the design process of websites, apps and technology and also also highlights that certain design choices made can either include or exclude certain groups of people of users. Considerations of design are usually centred around and cater for the default user who is able and not disadvantaged in any way. This is where design justice comes into play. Design justice means creating platforms that prioritise the people who are excluded. This made me reconsider my design choice and prioritise inclusivity. A simple element like a colour choice, your website colour palette or font size should be picked with the consideration of every type of user that will be accessing and engaging with your website. I used light colours for my font in content based sections such as blogs but later changed this as it affects readability.

Algorithms of Oppression

Safiya Noble focuses on the neutrality of search engines on the Internet. She makes an example of how google is not neutral and often suggests trending topics first while also reinforcing stereotypes and putting important voice to the side. Safiya terms this as “algorithmic oppression”, when algorithms follow major issues of oppression such as racism and sexism. An example of this is searching “Black girls” on google. There is harmful and inappropriate content under this that should be hidden under normal circumstances but still shows up which further proves Nobles point that the internet is indeed a harmful platform.

Both these articles made me go back to my design process and guide to make more ethical choices that promote accessibility and inclusivity.

References

Costanza-Chock, S. (2020). Design Justice: Community-Led Practices to Build the Worlds We Need. MIT Press. Noble, S. U. (2018). Algorithms of Oppression: How Search Engines Reinforce Racism. NYU Press.
