Introduction

This analysis will focus on the UI and UX principles identified on a website followed by points on ethical practices that should be considered during the design process of a website.

I have chosen to analyse the Takealot website which is a South African online company that deals with retail. I will be critically analysing their website while considering factors such as User Interface, User Experience as well as the ethical practices of design interaction. While analysing these factors, I will also be drawing on the readings and key concepts from Verdi’s The Road to Technocolonialism (2020) and Turing’s foundational work Computing Machinery and Intelligence (1950) to form a deeper understanding of how certain design decisions made can be linked to ideological and ethical frameworks.

Takealot is a South African ecommerce website which was first launched in the year 2011. People utilised the app the most in 2019, before and after the covid pandemic. It is known for its variety of products such as healthcare, electronics and clothing as well and its efficiency when it comes to delivery. Popular online services such Mr D (a food delivery company) and Superbalist (an online retail store) are products of Takealot and were branched from it in 2014. With the platform having a broad demographic as it does, it is vital for to have digital interactions that are ethical, inclusive and efficient to cater for the variation of products and services that they offer.

UI and UX fundamentals and analysis on Takealot

Website navigation and layout

Takealot follows a design structure like Amazon’s design structure because of its variation. The top navigation bar is persistant and gives access to important categories such as a search bar, your account, help centre and orders.

Despite the positive points mentioned above, there a few issues that the site has. The layout specifically on the homepage is content packed and may be overwhelming for users who are first time users and are not digitally inclined or literate. There are an overflow of banners and promotion highlights which can again, overwhelm users who are not familiar with a layout like this and can cause to lack direction in their navigation on this website. However, the grid-based structure does allow for easy scanning of content and products which falls under the principle of task-oriented browsing.

Another key principle of UI and UX is mobile responsiveness. The Takealot website excells in this and can adapt to different screen sizes of various of gadgets without it crashing. However, certain promotional elements especially on mobile devices can be viewed as intrusive such as pop-up messages that suggest users to download. This interferes with the users experience and further distract them from what they initially planned on doing.

The search bar is functional and is effective due to its auto-suggest and predictive text elements which makes it easier for users to look for specific products without wasting time. This feature caters for the user's intent and guides users easily but can also be bias as it showcases products that are the most popular first while putting products that may be relevant to what they may want last.

There are inclusive design principles that are not available such as voice-assisted navigation which further limits accessibility for specific users who prefer features of that sort. Reviews and product ratings are integrated separately but the sponsored content overpowers and shuts out the difference between anatomical and paid observability.

The checkout process is flawless is an easy-going process with clear direction and instructions while giving multiple options to pay such as, credit and debit cards, eft, cash on delivery and apple play/google pay. Despite this, ethical concerns have been raised about Takealot credit which could quickly turn into a negative habit that solely benefits business interests without considering user autonomy.

UI & UX strengths and weaknesses

  • Strengths:
  • Familiar and inherent layout for users who have experience in online shopping.
  • Well organised product scanning and filtering tools.
  • Good mobile responsiveness and logistical support.
  • Weaknesses:
  • Restricted accessibility features.
  • Over reliance on global design trends which leads to a lack of originality.
  • Biasness commercially in search due to sponsored content or products,
  • Deceptive design choices or suggestions during the checkout procedure.

Ethical Considerations in Design Interaction

Interaction design is often looked at as something autonomous because there are specific design choices that are looked down upon. Every element from button choices, shapes and colour choices do have influence on user behaviour which is they should be carefully considered. In this section, I will be focusing on how Takealot's interface includes ethical issues such accessibility and technological power.

The biggest issue on this sites interaction design that users have complained about the most is its accessibility. There are features available that may cater for people who have disabilities. There are no contrast tools for visually impaired individuals and a text-to-speech option available for users with disabilities which results in them being restricted to using the website to its fullest extent. This is viewed as digital exclusion which is an unethical practice as certain groups of people are left out because the focus is to cater for and prioritise the default users who have no disabilities. Usability in the digital world should not be viewed as a privillege but should also be right just like other rights that individuals have.

Online platforms are known for collecting behavioural data to improve and personalise the users experience according to what they like or find interest in. This has its pros and cons as ethical concerns may spiral about user content and what else this information is being used for. There is a lack of transparency when it comes to how the algorithms from data shape their shopping experience which mirrors concerns raised in Verdi’s (2020) review of technocolonialism where algorithms serve the purpose of controlling while also benefiting corporate interests first before consideration of the users.

Integration of Theoretical Frameworks from Verdi and Turing

Verdi (2020): Technocolonialism and Digital Power

Verdi’s argument states that the digital world is packed with colonial influence and makes use of the term “technocolonialism” to emphasise how much power digital platforms and systems use this to support universal biasness while portraying an image of innocence and innovation.

It is obvious that Takealot's design was taken and inspired by western e-commerce models instead of it being authentic and original in its structure especially as a South African platform. Verdi explains that this is colonial continuality as there is a cycle of observing what western countries and doing and copying their procedures especially when coming to data and information, instead of incorporating local cultures within this platform so that it stands out as a South African platform with features highlighting that it was established and launched by our own. An example of this being the language being limited to English only when it could have catered for all South African groups to emphasize diversity in language.

Turing (1950): Intelligence, Machines & Human Interaction

A prominent question that Turing asks is “Can machines think?” a question that made individuals critically think about the use of artificial intelligence. Takealot is a website and an app but not an Ai tool, its structure and features have similarities and mirror those of artificial intelligence tools. This is observed in its predictionary approach, recommendations and filtering tools as well.

Viewing this while considering Turing's reading, Takealot shows signs of machine thinking by personalising the users experience according to what they want and the checkout process too. Turing briefly explains that there should be a clear difference between machines assisting humans instead of taking over and controlling and unfortunately, this is where Takealot misses this.

Conclusion

The analysis of takealot.com while considering its UI and UX functionality reveals a complicated exchange between its efficiency and power. On a surface, this website is successful in terms of the factors such as the user experience while shopping, familiarity, responsiveness and the diversity of products being sold but these mask underlying issues within the interface that may raise political, social and ethical concerns.

Takealot prioritises the speedy sales of their products and overlook factors such as transparency and inclusivity. For example, the lack of features for individuals living with disabilities. Verdi and Tuning’s readings help us see how Takealot's design structures reinforces universal power by prioritising only one group of users and not considering the others. Tuning also states that artificial intelligence systems should be constructed in a way that they do not take over but assist and can be trusted by humans.

Ethical interactions consider so many factors like who the platform is for and who will be using it and not just focusing making a simple website without ethical depth. This case study carefully highlights that pros and cons of having a website like Takealot.
